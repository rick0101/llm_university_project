# VARK: Disentangling Prompt-Based Safety from Algorithmic Resilience in Large Language Models

## Abstract
The rapid deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) has necessitated a shift from heuristic safety measures to rigorous security frameworks. However, existing evaluations often fail to distinguish between the superficial protection provided by system-level safety prompts and the inherent robustness of algorithmic defense kernels. This confounding variable leads to a precarious "security through incompetence," where a model's failure to follow malicious instructions is misattributed to defensive strength. We introduce VARK (Validated Adversarial Resilience Kernels), a framework designed to isolate weight-level resilience through a systematic ablation of safety prompts and a multi-stage validation process. Our evaluation across three major defense categories—Randomized Smoothing, Adversarial Training, and Reinforcement Learning from Unsafe Feedback—reveals that while safety prompts provide initial coverage, base models exhibit a 0.125 Attack Success Rate (ASR) at a 0.2 noise threshold. In contrast, specialized algorithmic kernels maintain a 0.0 ASR across all tested noise regimes, providing a necessary secondary layer of defense. These findings underscore the importance of prompt-independent resilience for ensuring true security in adversarial environments.

## 1 Introduction
The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into autonomous decision-making pipelines and user-facing applications has expanded the attack surface for malicious exploitation. As these models evolve from passive text generators to agentic entities capable of interacting with external tools, the risks associated with adversarial triggers—ranging from prompt injection to gradient-based jailbreaking—have moved to the forefront of AI safety research [zou2023universal]. While the research community has proposed numerous defensive layers, including Randomized Smoothing [cohen2019certified], Adversarial Training [madry2018towards], and Reinforcement Learning from Unsafe Feedback (RLUF) [bai2022constitutional], the evaluation of these methods remains fragmented. A significant portion of contemporary security reporting relies on small-scale qualitative tests that fail to account for the confounding influence of system-level instructions, resulting in a distorted understanding of defensive efficacy.

A fundamental challenge in current LLM security research is the inability to disentangle the "Safety Prompt"—a natural language instruction to be helpful and harmless—from the underlying algorithmic defense mechanism. When a model like Llama-3 [touvron2023llama] or Phi-2 [li2023phi] refuses a malicious request, it is often unclear whether the refusal stems from the robust optimization of the weights or simply from the model’s adherence to its system-level instructions. This ambiguity is particularly problematic in small-parameter models, where "security through incompetence" may occur; the model might fail an attack not because it is defended, but because it lacks the instruction-following capability to execute the malicious task even without interference [casper2023open]. Furthermore, the statistical significance of many defensive claims is often questionable, as reported results are frequently derived from limited sample sizes where minor variations in token generation can lead to drastic shifts in perceived safety.

To address these methodological gaps, we propose the Validated Adversarial Resilience Kernel (VARK). VARK is a formalized framework designed to isolate and quantify the marginal contribution of algorithmic defenses independent of prompt-level interventions. By employing a 2x2 ablation matrix that crosses safety prompt status with defense kernel activation, VARK allows researchers to identify exactly where the heavy lifting of security occurs. Building on the principles of certified robustness [salman2019provably], our framework emphasizes the need for high-frequency stochastic testing to move beyond anecdotal evidence and provide a statistically grounded map of the adversarial landscape. This approach reveals that many perceived successes in LLM defense are fragile artifacts of prompt engineering that dissolve under rigorous, disentangled testing.

Building on this systematic foundation, we apply VARK to a suite of state-of-the-art defenses across varying noise thresholds.
