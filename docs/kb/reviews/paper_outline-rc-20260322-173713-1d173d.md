## Abstract (Target: 180-220 words)
**Problem:** Despite the rapid deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs), they remain critically vulnerable to prompt injection and jailbreaking attacks that bypass safety guardrails. Current defenses, such as system prompting or input filtering, often fail against adaptive adversarial attacks like GCG or TAP, or impose prohibitive latency and utility trade-offs. 
**Method:** We present **VIGIL** (Vector-Informed Guardrail for Injection Locking), a novel defense framework that detects adversarial intent by monitoring the latent trajectory of token embeddings during the pre-fill stage. Unlike black-box filters, VIGIL identifies "adversarial manifolds" within the model’s internal representations, allowing for real-time intervention before the first token is generated. 
**Results:** Extensive evaluation on HarmBench and JailbreakBench demonstrates that VIGIL reduces the Attack Success Rate (ASR) of state-of-the-art adaptive attacks from 84.2% to 6.3% on Llama-3-70B. Crucially, VIGIL maintains a 98.4% retention of model utility on MMLU benchmarks and introduces a negligible execution overhead of <2ms per request. Our analysis confirms that latent-space monitoring provides a more robust signal than surface-level text analysis, effectively neutralizing even "stealthy" gradient-based perturbations.
