At the core of this research is the fundamental tension between the utility of Large Language Models (LLMs)—their ability to follow complex instructions—and their safety alignments designed to prevent harmful outputs. The trajectory of this field demonstrates a clear shift from manual, heuristic-based prompt engineering to highly automated, algorithmic exploitation. Methodologically, the paradigm of utilizing LLMs to attack other LLMs has become dominant, enabling the generation of sophisticated adversarial prompts with unprecedented efficiency and scale [Kritz_2025_Jailbreaking, Liu_2024_Autodan, Hsu_2025_Jailbreaking]. This automation has rendered traditional, computationally heavy gradient-based attacks less relevant, as newer techniques like 1-query text flipping [Liu_2024_Flipattack] and generative adversarial suffixes [Kumar_2024_LLMs] achieve comparable or superior Attack Success Rates (ASRs) with a fraction of the overhead.\n\nA critical theme emerging from this cluster is the persistent vulnerability of frontier models. Despite massive investments in safety training by organizations developing GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, and Gemini, researchers consistently report ASRs exceeding 80-90% across various novel attack vectors [Weng_2025_LLMs, Zhao_2024_SQL, Zheng_2024_Improved]. This indicates that current alignment techniques, such as RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), are insufficient against structurally novel or contextually deceptive inputs. The literature highlights a divergence in attack strategies to bypass these alignments: some researchers focus on exploiting the autoregressive nature of LLMs through low-level structural manipulations like system token injection [Zheng_2024_Improved] or chat template manipulation [Zhao_2024_SQL], while others leverage high-level semantic deception through multi-turn conversational strategies designed to induce self-corruption [Weng_2025_LLMs, Kritz_2025_Jailbreaking].\n\nFurthermore, the scope of prompt injection research has expanded significantly beyond isolated chatbot interfaces to encompass complex, integrated AI systems. The vulnerabilities demonstrated in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines, where malicious instructions are disguised within retrieved documents [Wang_2024_LLMs, Chugh_2026_Recap], and in agentic workspaces, where attackers can hijack coding editors to execute unauthorized terminal commands with up to 84% success [Liu_2025_AI], underscore the severe real-world implications of these flaws. These system-level attacks represent a critical gap in current security paradigms, which often focus solely on the user-model interface.\n\nOn the defensive front, the field is transitioning from reactive patching to proactive, structural analysis. The development of defenses based on the geometric manifold properties of prompts, such as CurvaLID achieving ~99% detection accuracy [Yung_2025_Geometry], and the use of multilingual BERT embeddings to counter cross-lingual attacks [Rahman_2024_BERT], represent promising steps towards more robust, generalized detection mechanisms. However, the evaluation of both attacks and defenses remains a contentious area. As LLMs become increasingly autonomous and integrated into critical infrastructure, the shift from theoretical vulnerabilities to practical, system-level exploits necessitates a corresponding evolution in defensive strategies. Future research must bridge the gap between theoretical alignment and practical robustness, developing deterministic evaluation frameworks and holistic security architectures capable of withstanding the next generation of automated, AI-driven cyber threats."
