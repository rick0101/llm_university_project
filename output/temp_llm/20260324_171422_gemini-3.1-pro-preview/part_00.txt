Moving far beyond early theoretical vulnerabilities in simple text classifiers, contemporary research demonstrates that highly sophisticated, stealthy backdoors can be injected into Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and specialized AI agents (such as GUI and VLA systems) with alarming ease. A defining convergence in this field is the remarkably low poisoning budget required; adversaries can achieve near 100% Attack Success Rates using as little as 0.1% poisoned data [Mamun_2025_LLMs, Zhang_2024_Persistent] or a mere 250 documents in large-scale pre-training [Souly_2025_Poisoning]. By leveraging Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and two-stage optimization strategies, attackers can embed complex malicious behaviors—ranging from reasoning manipulation [Shen_2025_Decepchain] and latency degradation [Li_2026_Slowba] to targeted refusal and copyright elicitation [Panaitesculiess_2025_Poisonedparrot]—without degrading the model's performance on benign tasks. Furthermore, the threat landscape has expanded to include autonomous LLM agents capable of crafting highly evasive semantic triggers [Li_2025_Autobackdoor], as well as inference-time attacks that bypass the training pipeline entirely [Wang_2026_Badtemplate]. Perhaps most concerning is the proven persistence of these backdoors; they not only survive rigorous safety alignment processes like SFT and DPO [Zhang_2024_Persistent] but can also amplify during model distillation [Chaudhari_2025_Cascading]. Given that standard defensive mechanisms such as perplexity filtering and model pruning consistently fail against these advanced multimodal and semantic threats, this synthesis underscores an urgent need for a paradigm shift in AI security, requiring novel, robust defensive frameworks capable of securing the entire lifecycle of increasingly agentic and interconnected AI systems."
