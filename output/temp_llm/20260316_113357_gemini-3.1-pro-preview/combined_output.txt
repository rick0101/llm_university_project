As highlighted by the foundational taxonomy in [Chowdhury_2024_Breaking], the initial surge of LLM adoption between 2021 and 2023 exposed a myriad of vulnerabilities, necessitating structured frameworks to understand threats ranging from prompt injection to data poisoning. Recognizing that traditional backdoor attacks from the classification era were inadequate for generative models, the field moved to standardize evaluations through comprehensive benchmarks like BackdoorLLM [Li_2024_Backdoorllm]. Concurrently, researchers identified the severe computational costs and catastrophic forgetting associated with traditional fine-tuning attacks. Addressing this, [Li_2024_Badedit] introduced a paradigm shift by reframing backdoor injection as a lightweight knowledge editing problem, demonstrating that direct manipulation of specific MLP layers can achieve near-perfect attack success with minimal data and zero degradation of benign capabilities. Pushing the frontier further into 2025, the focus expanded beyond single-modal text generation to the complex alignment phases of multi-modal architectures. [Duan_2025_RLHF] exposed critical vulnerabilities in the Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) pipeline of Text-to-Image models, utilizing clean-label feature collisions in the CLIP latent space to stealthily corrupt reward models. Together, this literature illustrates a continuous arms race: as models grow in scale and modality, adversaries are moving away from brute-force data poisoning toward highly efficient, targeted parameter edits and stealthy alignment subversion, leaving the development of robust, generalizable defenses as an urgent and open challenge for the research community."
